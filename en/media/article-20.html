Does “Staying Energetic Requires Intense Exercise” Really Hold Up? What Recent Insights Teach Us About Breath-Focused ‘Yoga-Style Habits’ and Healthy Ageing

Published: July 11, 2026  |  Source published: June 19, 2026

“To stay energetic, you have to exercise hard enough to really sweat” — many people may believe this. In June 2026, an event marking the United Nations' “International Day of Yoga” was held at Tsukiji Hongwan-ji Temple in Tokyo and drew attention. Here's a perspective for midlife readers and beyond, drawn from this year's theme: “Yoga for Healthy Ageing.”

Three Points From the Event

  • This year's theme, “Yoga for Healthy Ageing,” frames health not as “the absence of illness” but as living each day with vitality, the event explains. In Japan's long-living society, the importance of leading a full and authentic life, in mind and body, as you age is highlighted.
  • Yoga is described as a practice passed down in India for over 5,000 years — not merely exercise, but something that values harmony among body, breath, and mind. Derived from a Sanskrit word meaning “to unite,” it has traditionally emphasized harmony between breath and posture over vigorous movement, the event notes.
  • International Yoga Day was formally established by the United Nations in 2014, and around the world it has become an occasion to reaffirm the value of health and well-being, the event explains. This event was also held in an accessible, free, registration-based format.

What We, in Midlife, Should Keep in Mind

What this event conveys is that healthy ageing may call for gentle time spent attending to breath and posture, more than intense exercise. You don't need to attend a yoga class or gather special equipment — a similar feeling can be brought into daily life. The following commonly recommended habits may serve as a starting point.

  • In the morning, try a few slow, deep breaths while sitting on a chair or in bed
  • Take a few minutes during the day to sit up straight and relax your shoulders
  • Before bed, gently stretch while paying attention to the rhythm of your breath

* The above are commonly recommended lifestyle habits and do not guarantee improvement, treatment, or prevention of any specific condition.
