Are “Hip and Knee Trouble Just About Age” Really True? What a Recent Report Teaches Us About the Hip Joint and the Body's Foundation

Published: June 25, 2026  |  Source published: June 21, 2026

When you feel heaviness in your lower back or discomfort in your knees, it's easy to think, “that's just age.” But in June 2026, an explainer from a sports clinic drew attention by suggesting that hip joints stiffened by long periods of sitting may be part of the picture. Here's an easy-to-follow summary for readers in midlife and beyond.

Three Points the Report Makes

  • Lower back heaviness and knee discomfort are often linked to reduced hip flexibility, the explainer notes. Because joints and muscles move together through what's called the “kinetic chain,” a stiff hip can mean that impact which should be absorbed there instead shifts to the lower back above and the knee below.
  • While seated, the inner muscles at the front of the hip (such as the iliopsoas) tend to shorten and stiffen, the explainer points out. If those muscles don't fully lengthen when you stand up, the pelvis gets pulled forward, and the body compensates for balance by arching the lower back, it explains.
  • In professional sports settings, maintaining hip mobility is treated as thorough prevention, the explainer notes. Rather than addressing pain after it appears, the idea introduced is to keep joints moving regularly to prevent uneven strain from building up in the first place.

What We, in Midlife, Should Keep in Mind

This explainer isn't saying that age-related change doesn't happen at all — it's a reminder not to attribute discomfort to age alone, and to also pay attention to the body's foundation, starting with the hip joints. The following commonly recommended habits are said to help maintain hip flexibility.

  • During long stretches of sitting, stand up and move gently about once an hour
  • While watching TV or videos, spend a little time opening and closing the hips while seated, within a comfortable range
  • Avoid an arched lower back or hunched posture, and keep the hips free to move smoothly

* The above are commonly recommended general health habits and do not guarantee improvement or treatment of any specific condition.
