Is “Noodles Alone Are Fine When You Have No Summer Appetite” Just an Assumption? What Recent Insights Teach Us About Protein and Preserving Muscle Mass in Summer

Published: July 16, 2026  |  Source published: July 11, 2026

When the heat kills your appetite, it's easy to get by on just noodles — cold somen or hiyashi chuka. Many people probably recognize this pattern in their own summer meals. In July 2026, Taiya Naika Clinic in Toyota City, Aichi Prefecture, drew attention by announcing a health cooking class led by a registered dietitian, titled “Meals That Don't Let You Lose Muscle, Even in Summer.” Prompted by this initiative, here's a look at how to approach summer eating habits, useful for midlife readers and beyond.

Three Points the Cooking Class Makes

  • Protein intake is positioned as key to staying healthy precisely during the summer months, when appetite tends to drop. A diet skewed toward cold noodles can lead to protein deficiency without you noticing, potentially contributing to a decline in muscle mass, the explainer notes.
  • The class is designed around a registered dietitian teaching high-protein cooking techniques that are easy to recreate at home. It's said to be designed with everyday accessibility in mind, requiring no special ingredients or difficult cooking skills.
  • This is a clinic specializing in diabetes and lifestyle-related diseases, hosting the event not just for treatment but also as part of prevention and health promotion for local residents. A cooking class format is described as offering an enjoyable opportunity to reconsider eating habits.

What We, in Midlife, Should Keep in Mind Day to Day

This cooking class conveys that “having no appetite in summer means noodles alone will have to do” isn't the only option — a little extra effort can help prevent protein deficiency. The following commonly recommended tips may offer a starting point for revisiting your summer eating habits.

  • Don't rely on noodles alone — add a protein-rich dish, such as egg, tofu, meat, or fish
  • During stretches of cold dishes, try seasonings with herbs or acidity to make food easier to enjoy
  • Rather than trying to get a lot at once, aim for a little protein at each meal

* The above are commonly recommended dietary habits and do not guarantee improvement of any specific condition or change in muscle mass.
