Is “Summer Fatigue Is Just About Willpower” Really True? What Recent Insights Teach Us About ‘Sour Cravings’ in Extreme Heat and Fermented-Food Habits

Published: July 10, 2026  |  Source published: June 24, 2026

“Summer fatigue happens because you lack willpower or stamina” — many people probably picture it this way. In June 2026, a media seminar on the physical changes that occur during extreme heat drew attention. Here's an easy-to-follow summary for readers in midlife and beyond.

Three Points the Seminar Makes

  • Extreme heat is described as placing a burden not just on fatigue levels but on immune function as well. As skin-surface temperature rises, the immune antibodies that help defend against viral invasion may work less effectively; as blood is drawn toward the skin to release heat, blood flow around the gut may temporarily decrease, the seminar explains.
  • Craving something sour in summer is presented as more than a matter of taste — it may be a signal from the body. A researcher who spoke at the event explained that the desire for sourness involves both food culture and the body, and that there's reasoning behind the long-held wisdom of “eat vinegared dishes in summer.”
  • “Unfiltered vinegar” is introduced as belonging to the same family of fermented foods as miso and natto. Data was also presented showing that household vinegar consumption has been declining compared to before, a trend particularly notable among younger generations.

What We, in Midlife, Should Keep in Mind

This seminar conveys that when the heat takes away your appetite, skillfully bringing sourness and fermentation into your meals may help. Practical cooking tips were also shared — for example, adding vinegar earlier rather than at the very end of cooked dishes is said to mellow the sourness and add depth of flavor. The following commonly recommended habits are said to help you manage your condition in summer.

  • Even when your appetite dips, try adding a vinegared side dish or a dish that uses vinegar
  • In simmered dishes and soups, try adding vinegar earlier in cooking rather than at the very end
  • Bring fermented foods — miso, natto, amazake, vinegar — into your meals in a balanced way

* The above are commonly recommended dietary habits and do not guarantee improvement, treatment, or prevention of any specific condition.
