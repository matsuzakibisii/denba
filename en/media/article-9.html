Is “Freezing Makes It Safe” Really True? What an Endoscopist's Explainer Teaches Us About the Hidden Gaps in Food-Poisoning Prevention From Rainy Season Into Summer

Published: June 29, 2026  |  Source published: June 21, 2026

“It's in the fridge, so it should be fine,” or “I froze it, so any bacteria must be dead by now” — many of us rely on these kinds of homegrown food-safety rules through the rainy season and into summer. But in June 2026, an explainer from the director of a gastroenterology and endoscopy clinic drew attention by suggesting that some of these well-meaning precautions actually have overlooked gaps. Here's an easy-to-follow summary for readers in midlife and beyond.

Three Points the Explainer Makes

  • Undercooking raw meat or fish raises the risk of bacterial food poisoning, the explainer notes. As temperature and humidity rise going forward, bacteria such as Salmonella and Campylobacter multiply more easily, so it's important to cook meat thoroughly all the way through, it explains.
  • Overstuffing the refrigerator is pointed out as an easily overlooked blind spot. Packing the fridge too tightly disrupts air circulation and can raise the internal temperature, so the explainer recommends keeping it around 70% full and handling raw meat and fish last during food prep.
  • A home freezer (around −18°C) is not guaranteed to fully kill the Anisakis parasite, the explainer states. Reliably killing it requires either a lower temperature maintained for a set period, or thorough cooking, it explains.

What We, in Midlife, Should Keep in Mind

This explainer isn't saying that refrigeration or freezing alone is enough for peace of mind — it's a reminder that revisiting each step, from cooking to storage, helps keep food poisoning at bay. Dehydration and reduced stamina can affect midlife bodies more noticeably, and the following commonly recommended habits are said to help keep your gut healthy.

  • When cooking meat or fish, make sure it's thoroughly cooked through and avoid undercooking
  • Keep the fridge around 70% full so cold air can circulate properly
  • If you feel unwell, prioritize hydration and don't force yourself to eat

* The above are commonly recommended general health habits and do not guarantee improvement or treatment of any specific condition. If diarrhea or vomiting is severe, or you feel uneasy, please don't push through it — consult a medical institution.
